SVD Chapter 4: Where You Have Already Been Using It

Four operations came out of chapters 1–3: count the directions, compare biggest to smallest, budget by σ², truncate. This chapter is the same four, wearing different clothes — PCA, LoRA, denoising, and the optimizer you already read.

PCA is the SVD of your data, with the mean removed

The setup, and the one preparatory step

Put your data in a matrix X: one row per sample, one column per feature. Subtract each column's mean — that is the only preparation, and it matters because SVD measures stretch from the origin, so an off-centre cloud would report its own position instead of its shape.

Then take the SVD of the centred X and read off the pieces:

SVD objectPCA namemeaning
vᵢ (rows of Vᵀ)principal component ia direction in feature space along which the data spreads
σᵢ² / (n−1)variance explainedhow much of the spread lies along it

A run, so the correspondence is not just a claim

200 samples, 2 correlated features:

output — real run singular values of centred X: [32.767, 4.103] variance explained: [98.5%, 1.5%] first principal direction v₁ = [−0.9008, −0.4342] eigenvalues of the covariance XᵀX/(n−1): [5.3953, 0.0846] σ²/(n−1) from the SVD: [5.3953, 0.0846] identical

Two features, but 98.5% of the variation lies along a single direction — so this data is really one number plus a little noise. That is chapter 3's "fast-decaying spectrum means structure," applied to data instead of to a matrix of weights.

Why practitioners use SVD rather than eigendecomposing the covariance: forming XᵀX squares the condition number (chapter 3, page 1), so you throw away half your precision before you start. Running the SVD on X directly skips that. It is the same answer, computed more safely.

LoRA: a bet that the update you need is low-rank

The construction

Fine-tuning wants to add an update ΔW to a frozen weight W. LoRA never stores ΔW. It stores two thin matrices and multiplies them:

ΔW = B · A B is (d × r), A is (r × d), r ≪ d rank(BA) ≤ min(rank B, rank A) ≤ r # the inner dimension caps it

So r is literally the rank from chapter 3, chosen in advance. Verified on a toy: B(50×8) @ A(8×50) has shape 50×50 and rank exactly 8.

The saving is the whole point:

output — real run: one 4096×4096 weight full ΔW 16,777,216 params r = 8 B+A = 65,536 0.391% r = 16 B+A = 131,072 0.781% r = 64 B+A = 524,288 3.125%

Why it works at all — and what the bet is

Chapter 3 said a matrix is compressible exactly when its spectrum decays fast. LoRA's wager is that the ΔW fine-tuning needs has a fast-decaying spectrum — that adapting a pretrained model is a low-dimensional change, not a rewrite. If true, a rank-r factorization loses almost nothing (Eckart–Young bounds the loss by σr+1). If false, no choice of r helps.

Note the direction of the logic: LoRA does not compute an SVD. It fixes the rank up front and trains B and A directly. The SVD is what tells you why that could ever be enough — and what "r is too small" would look like (a spectrum still tall at index r).

It also explains the standard practice of raising r when a task fails to fit: you are buying more directions. And why you cannot compare an r=8 adapter's capacity to a full fine-tune's — one is constrained to an 8-dimensional slice of the update space.

Compression and denoising are the same act, judged differently

A signal with a known rank, buried in noise

Build a 40×40 matrix that is genuinely rank 2, add gaussian noise, and look at what the spectrum does:

output — real run clean signal σ: [110.495, 15.674, 0.000, 0.000, 0.000] rank 2 noisy σ: [109.921, 15.795, 4.739, 4.312, 4.109] rank 40 ↖—— signal ——↗ ↖—— the noise floor ——↗

The noise did two things: it barely moved the two real singular values, and it lifted every other one off zero to about 4.3. The gap between 15.8 and 4.7 is the signal/noise boundary, visible directly in the spectrum — that "elbow" is what people are looking for when they plot singular values and squint.

Now truncate, and watch the error go the wrong way

output — real run: reconstruct from the top k, compare to the CLEAN signal keep k= 1: error = 16.112 # too few — a real direction is missing keep k= 2: error = 5.237 # the true rank — best possible keep k= 3: error = 7.064 # worse! keep k= 5: error = 9.239 keep k=10: error = 12.375 # each extra direction re-adds noise

Past k=2, every direction you keep makes the answer worse. That is the striking part: throwing information away improves accuracy, because the information you threw away was noise. Truncating the SVD is a denoiser, and the optimal cut is at the elbow.

Same operation, two names, depending on what you were after:

you keep the top k because…you call it
you wanted the structure and don't want to store the restcompression
you wanted the structure and believe the rest is corruptiondenoising
you wanted the structure and the rest is other people's variationPCA / dimensionality reduction

Back to the optimizer — and the whole picture

Muon, now readable line by line

Optimizer chapter 7 is an SVD chapter in disguise. With chapters 1–3 done, every step of it should now decode:

Muon saysyou now read it as
"gradient updates are nearly rank-1"the spectrum is [56.7, 0.82, 0.14] — one direction holds essentially all the energy (ch. 3 p2)
"condition number is very high"κ = 411 — the output ellipse is a sliver (ch. 3 p1)
"replace M with U·Vᵀ"set every σ to 1: keep both rotations, delete the stretch (ch. 2 p0)
"Newton–Schulz acts on singular values only"p(USVᵀ) = U·φ(S)·Vᵀ, because UᵀU = VᵀV = I (ch. 2 p0's identity, used twice)
"the rare directions are drowned, not absent"σ₃ = 0.14 ≠ 0 — nonzero, so it is real signal at the wrong scale (ch. 3 p0)

The one-sentence version: Muon computes the SVD's answer without computing the SVD, because doing the decomposition per weight per step would be far too slow.

Two more places it turns up

  • Least squares and the pseudo-inverse. To solve Mx = b when M isn't invertible, invert what you can: M⁺ = V S⁺ Uᵀ, where S⁺ replaces each nonzero σ by 1/σ and leaves zeros alone. The zeros are the directions that were crushed — you cannot un-crush them, so the pseudo-inverse simply declines to. This is also why near-zero σ's are dangerous: 1/σ explodes, which is chapter 3's condition number arriving as a concrete numerical failure.
  • Whitening. Replace every σ by 1 and the ellipse becomes a circle — κ = 1, every direction equal. That is exactly what Muon does to a gradient, and what whitening does to data before training.

The whole course, four lines

1Every matrix maps the unit circle to an ellipse. Its four numbers (axes + lengths) are the matrix, re-expressed as behaviour instead of storage.
2M vᵢ = σᵢ uᵢ, stacked into columns, is M = U S Vᵀ. The σ's are √eigenvalues of MᵀM — verified by hand: 45 and 5 → 6.7082 and 2.2361.
3Count them (rank), divide the biggest by the smallest (κ), square them for a budget (σ² sums to ‖M‖²F), truncate them (best rank-k, error exactly σk+1).
4PCA, LoRA, compression, denoising, pseudo-inverse, whitening, Muon — each is one of those four operations wearing a domain's clothes.

Questions worth being able to answer

questionone-line answer
What is the SVD, in one sentence?Every matrix is a rotation, then an axis-aligned stretch, then another rotation — equivalently, it maps the unit circle to an ellipse, and U, S, V are that ellipse's axes and lengths.
Why does every matrix have one, when not every matrix has eigenvectors?Because it comes from MᵀM, which is always square, symmetric and PSD — so real non-negative eigenvalues and perpendicular eigenvectors are guaranteed, even when M is non-square.
Singular values vs eigenvalues?Different questions. For our M: eigenvalues 3 and 5, singular values 6.7082 and 2.2361. They coincide only for symmetric PSD matrices. σᵢ = √λᵢ(MᵀM).
Why is truncating optimal?Eckart–Young: no rank-k matrix is closer to M in Frobenius norm, and the error is exactly σk+1 — checked here: ‖M − A₁‖ = 2.236068 = σ₂.
How do you choose k?Look for the elbow in the spectrum — the gap between signal and noise floor (110, 15.8 ‖ 4.7, 4.3, 4.1). Or keep enough for a target share of ∑σ².
Why is PCA usually done by SVD instead of eigendecomposing the covariance?Forming XᵀX squares the condition number, costing half your precision. SVD on X gives the same answer without that.
What does a high condition number cost?κ = 10ᵏ loses roughly k digits; it also sets the zigzag rate for gradient descent, and κ(AᵀA) = κ(A)².
Why is a batch gradient low-rank?It is a sum of B outer products (one per example), and rank(sum) ≤ B — so a batch of 32 gives a 4096×4096 weight at most 32 directions.
